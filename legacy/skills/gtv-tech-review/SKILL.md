# GTV Application Review

Review the supplied documents constructively and skeptically, then give a clear,
evidence-based recommendation. Scale depth to the requested scope and the material
issues; no prescribed pass count or quota of criticisms is required.

## Boundaries and current requirements

Never rewrite submitted prose or provide replacement sentences. Give findings,
factual gaps, questions, and revision goals. The applicant authors their documents;
each recommender independently authors their own letter. Verify Tech Nation's current
authorship guidance before submission advice and disclose if it cannot be retrieved.

Prose style cannot establish authorship or AI use. Report generic, unsupported,
templated, duplicated, or inconsistent passages only when they materially affect
credibility or clarity. Do not label text AI-generated or assign authorship probabilities.

Read [review-criteria.md](references/review-criteria.md) for applicable document checks
and official sources. Verify current criteria, limits, process, fees, and timing
against GOV.UK and Appendix Global Talent before relying on them. Use the Rules' four
optional criteria; innovation's two routes are not independent criteria. Reuse current
applicable source checks and clearly distinguish unavailable information from failure.

## Review the available scope

Accept pasted text, individual documents, partial submissions, or a complete set.
Use an MVE planning artifact if supplied, but do not require it. Resolve pathway and
target criteria from context. If uncertain, state a provisional interpretation and
ask only where it changes the review; continue independent substantive feedback.

Cover the relevant dimensions together:

- **Requirements:** current form prompts and limits, CV and letter requirements,
  evidence counts and assignment, and founder/executive proof where applicable.
- **Evidence and argument:** criterion fit, attributable impact, specificity,
  significance, corroboration, truthful UK plans, and distinctive supported facts.
- **Skeptical scrutiny:** the strongest supportable objections and missing proof,
  without invented questions, outcome predictions, or imagined assessor preferences.
- **Authorship and voice:** observable problems within the limits above; different
  recommenders may legitimately have different voices.
- **Consistency:** dates, names, metrics, contribution claims, each author's knowledge,
  and alignment with supplied planning notes.

For a single document, check internal consistency and state the limits of cross-document
review. Do not treat documents absent from a deliberately partial request as missing
from the actual application. Rate supplied documents Strong, Adequate, Needs Work, or
Weak using the reference's evidence standard, and rank material fixes ahead of polish.
A sound document can survive scrutiny without manufactured objections.

## Deliver and iterate

Use [review-report-template.md](references/review-report-template.md) as an adaptable
report structure. Include the reviewed scope, supporting passages, strengths, material
issues, consistency findings, sources checked, and prioritized revision goals. Omit
irrelevant or repetitive sections. Give a recommendation within the available scope:

- **Submit after edits:** remaining fixes are clarity or completeness; if none remain,
  say no material edits were identified rather than inventing work.
- **Revise before submission:** existing evidence has material presentation,
  consistency, corroboration, or responsible-authorship concerns.
- **Build evidence first:** a target criterion lacks credible evidence or support.

A partial review does not establish whole-application readiness. State that limit
instead of forcing a submission verdict about unseen documents.

For revisions, assess changed material and any connected claims it affects; reuse
still-applicable findings rather than repeating the entire review. Retire resolved
issues. If the prior version is unavailable, distinguish user-reported improvements
from changes verified by comparison and review the current document on its own merits.
Complete the report without editing documents, submitting an application, or messaging
recommenders. Explain meaningful evidence limits without implying legal advice or a
guaranteed outcome.
